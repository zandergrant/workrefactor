Keep requests in one place

This workbook gives your team a shared place to record requests, owners, statuses and due dates, along with a dashboard and realistic sample records.

The simplest useful operations tool is often one place where people can see what came in, who owns it, when it is due and what happens next. If requests move through email, chat and meetings, people end up rebuilding that picture every time someone asks for an update.

This workbook gives a small team a starting point: a clean request register, owner and priority fields, a dashboard, and an explicit set of statuses. It works in Excel and can be imported into Google Sheets.

The workbook includes sample requests that are clearly marked as fictional. Delete or replace them before using it for your own work. Dashboard metrics are formula-based and will update as records change, subject to your spreadsheet application's formula support.

Five simple rules to start with

  1. Every request has an ID. Avoid referring to a task only by a subject line or a person's name.
  2. One person owns the next action. Collaborators can be many; the accountable owner should be unambiguous.
  3. Statuses mean something. Use New for untriaged, Assigned for work ready to start, In Progress for active work, Blocked for work waiting on a dependency, and Complete only when the outcome is verified.
  4. A due date is a commitment, not decoration. If the date changes, record the reason and tell the affected person.
  5. The record is updated where the work happens. Don't make someone maintain a tracker and then write an entirely separate weekly status email from memory.

A 15-minute weekly check-in

Filter out completed items. Review everything overdue or blocked. Confirm that each new request has an owner. Check the next-action field for vague statements like “follow up.” Replace them with a person, a specific action and a date. Close items whose outcome has been confirmed. Archive older completed work at a regular interval rather than letting the active register grow indefinitely.

Keep it simple at first

Avoid five levels of priority, dozens of custom statuses, color codes that mean nothing, or separate tabs for every department. Each additional field should either affect a decision, enable a report, or help the next person complete the work. If it doesn't, it is likely extra administration.

Add more only when your team needs it

A workbook may be all your team needs while requests are easy to enter, find and update. If intake becomes inconsistent, a form might help; if people repeatedly need the same summaries, a dashboard may be worth adding. When staff need to update requests without working in the raw spreadsheet, a focused interface can make that easier. The spreadsheet-to-app demonstration shows that progression.

Important: A spreadsheet is not a permissions system. Do not put confidential client information into a broadly shared file; restrict access and review your organization's data-handling requirements.

A tracker is only part of the operating system. The business systems guide explains responsibility, escalation and authoritative records.

When one tracker is no longer enough

If requests arrive through several systems and nobody can rely on the status, we can help decide whether the right fix is a cleaner tracker, a simple integration or a focused interface.
