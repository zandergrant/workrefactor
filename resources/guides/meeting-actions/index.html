Turn meetings into decisions and actions

A simple record for decisions, owners and actions, with a responsible way to add AI-assisted extraction.

Meetings become expensive when the conversation ends and everyone has a different understanding of what was decided. Notes exist, but the next action is buried between discussion points. The team meets again to reconstruct what happened last time.

The solution is not necessarily an AI meeting assistant. Start with a consistent record of decisions and actions. Add AI only if it makes a reliable process easier to maintain.

Download the decision and action tracker · Download the meeting recap template

During the meeting

Keep a visible list with two different record types:

  • Decision: What was agreed, who authorized it, when it applies and where the rationale lives.
  • Action: The work someone committed to doing, with one owner, due date and definition of done.

“Look into the reporting problem” is a conversation topic. “Morgan will compare the two reporting exports and identify the mismatched fields by Thursday” is an action someone can complete and verify.

An AI-assisted version, with human review

If your organization permits AI processing of the meeting information, provide the transcript or notes and ask the model to separate explicit decisions from proposed ideas. Use a structured instruction like this:

Extract only decisions explicitly agreed and actions explicitly assigned in these notes. For each action, return the exact source passage, owner, due date, and a short completion condition. If the owner or date is not stated, write Needs confirmation rather than inferring it. Separate unresolved questions from decisions. Do not invent commitments.

Have the meeting owner review the output before anyone receives an automatic assignment. AI can produce confident but inaccurate summaries, especially when participants discuss options without choosing one.

A sensible automation

After approval, a script can create or update tasks in the team's existing tracker, retain a stable meeting-and-item ID to avoid duplicates, and send one concise summary with links to the relevant records. Log failed updates rather than silently dropping them. If the meeting is sensitive, use a manual workflow or an organization-approved system with appropriate controls.

Measure whether it helps

Track the share of action items that have an owner and date by the end of the meeting, overdue actions, duplicate assignments, and how often people ask for clarification afterward. Don't equate more extracted tasks with better work. The goal is fewer lost commitments and less re-discussion.
