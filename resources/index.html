Start with the work, then decide what needs to change.

When a process becomes frustrating, I start with a few recent examples of the work itself, looking for repeated entry, waiting, and decisions that nobody clearly owns. Once those patterns are visible, it becomes easier to agree on what should change and whether any new technology is needed.

I put these resources together so teams can try that approach in their own work. Take what is useful, adapt it to the way your team operates, and keep the working version somewhere people can find and update it.

Tools to diagnose, decide, and test a change.

The resources most teams should start with come first: look at where work gets stuck, weigh an automation opportunity, test one change, then clarify the handoffs and decisions around it. Each download is editable and requires no account.

01

Workflow audit worksheet

CSV spreadsheet

Trace a recurring process through each handoff, noting who owns it, where it waits, and how often the same information is entered again. Begin with three recent cases rather than an entire department.

Compare active time with waiting time, then test the smallest change that removes repeated effort.

02

Automation opportunity tracker

CSV spreadsheet

Record how frequently a repetitive task occurs, how much effort it consumes, which systems it touches, and the exceptions an automation would need to handle.

Consider whether you can remove the task before automating it, then choose one small pilot with a measurable outcome.

Small process experiment

Editable document

Use this brief to capture a recurring problem, choose one change, and compare what happened against the original baseline. It gives the team something concrete to review before deciding whether to keep the new approach.

Set your baseline and success measure before the test, then decide whether to keep, revise or stop.

04

Client handoff brief

Editable document

Capture what was promised, who is taking over, which records are authoritative, and what still needs to be supplied before the next team can start. The template works across sales, delivery and support.

Try it against a handoff that was recently delayed, and remove any fields the receiving team never uses.

05

Decision ownership and escalation map

CSV spreadsheet

When work keeps circling between people, the problem is often that nobody is sure who can make the next decision. Map recurring decisions, the owner, the limits of their authority and exactly when a case needs escalation.

Use a recent delayed approval or exception, agree who should decide, and test whether the next case moves without another round of status messages.

06

One-page SOP template

Editable document

A short standard operating procedure that records the trigger, owner, outcome, ordinary steps, and what to do when an exception arises. It is designed to be updated during real work, not filed away after a workshop.

Have someone who did not write the document follow it on a real case. Their questions show you what is missing.

Record the accepted answer or decision, the model's suggestion, corrections, missed escalations and the time needed to finish a case. The sheet is intended for supervised pilots, where someone reviews the result before it affects real work.

Will this automation save enough to justify the cost?

Start with what the work costs today, then estimate what it would take after automation, including review and corrections. The calculator subtracts maintenance time and recurring software costs, and also shows a cautious scenario where you achieve only half the estimated time savings.

These figures are illustrative, so replace them with observations from your own workflow.

The figures are estimates of capacity value, not promised cash savings. A shorter process does not necessarily reduce payroll expense, and this calculation does not quantify quality changes, failure risk, or the benefits of faster service. Test a few real cases before investing.

Questions about the templates.

Are these resources really free?

Everything here is free to download and adapt for your business, with no account or email address required.

Which template should I use first?

Begin with the workflow audit if you are still diagnosing a problem. Use the SOP or handoff brief for work you already understand, and test automation only after you have a baseline.

Can I open the worksheets in Excel or Google Sheets?

The CSV worksheets can be opened in Excel or Google Sheets, while the Markdown document templates can be edited as text or copied into a document editor.

Does the automation ROI calculator show actual savings?

It is an estimate based on your inputs. Time released is not automatically a reduction in expenses, and the financial result needs to be checked against a real pilot.

Looking for a more detailed explanation?

The templates are designed to be used immediately, while the longer guides cover the decisions behind them. Explore the tools above, or read Business Systems to understand process ownership and operating design, Workflow Automation for repeatable handoffs and exception handling, or Business Process Automation when the work crosses several teams. The AI for Operations and Internal Tools guides help with testing new technology without taking on unnecessary complexity.

Need help implementing the change?

If a worksheet reveals a process that is costing the team time, tell us where it gets stuck. We can help understand the work, run a manageable test, and build what is worth keeping.
