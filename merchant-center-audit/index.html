Common Merchant Center issues

Understanding the warning you've received.

A product disapproval, a website-related warning and an account-level enforcement action are not interchangeable. We start with the exact issue shown in Merchant Center and look for evidence relevant to that issue.

Misrepresentation warnings

We review whether the storefront clearly explains who operates the business, what customers are buying, how payment and fulfillment work, and where to find contact and policy information. A missing detail is not automatically proof of misrepresentation; the account notice and context matter.

Product disapprovals

We compare affected product examples with the product pages and information supplied to Merchant Center. Common areas to check include mismatched prices or availability, unclear product details and inconsistent landing pages.

Account suspensions and policy warnings

We examine the notice you received and identify which issues can be checked from the public website, which require account diagnostics and which must ultimately be resolved through Google's review process.

Shipping, returns and checkout inconsistencies

We trace what a shopper sees from product page to checkout, including delivery costs, return conditions, currency and availability. The goal is to identify conflicting or incomplete information, not to invent requirements that do not apply to your store.

Before you request an audit

Five checks you can make yourself.

These checks won't determine whether Google will approve your products. They can, however, help you collect the right evidence and identify obvious inconsistencies before asking for a second opinion.

  1. Read the exact issue notice.Record the issue name, affected items, destination and whether it applies to the account or individual products. Avoid treating a generic error summary as a complete diagnosis.
  2. Compare one affected product.Check that the landing page reflects the price, currency, availability, title and product details submitted in the feed or platform integration.
  3. Review business contact details.Make sure customers can identify and contact the business, and that customer-facing information is consistent across relevant pages.
  4. Walk through shipping and returns.Check whether customers can understand delivery costs, timelines, return conditions and any limitations before purchasing.
  5. Document what changed.Keep a dated record of relevant edits, screenshots and account notices. It helps distinguish a newly introduced mismatch from an issue that remains unresolved.

A public storefront check cannot reveal every private account signal or explain Google's internal enforcement decision. Repeatedly requesting reviews without addressing the underlying issue may not help.

Audit versus remediation

Understanding the scope of the audit.

Some merchants need help identifying a likely problem. Others already know what needs changing but lack the time or technical capacity to make the updates. We treat those as different engagements.

Compliance audit

A defined review of the notice, representative product information and relevant storefront pages.

  • Issue-specific findings
  • Evidence and confidence level
  • Prioritized recommended corrections
  • Unresolved questions and next steps

Does not include editing your store, submitting appeals or guaranteeing a result.

Optional remediation

Separately scoped assistance implementing changes identified during the review.

  • Customer-facing policy or information updates
  • Product page or feed consistency fixes
  • Workflow or integration improvements where appropriate
  • Documentation of completed changes

Availability, price and permissions are agreed before any implementation.

Common questions

What the audit can and cannot do.

Can you guarantee approval or account reinstatement?

No. Google makes all approval, enforcement and reinstatement decisions. We provide an independent assessment and recommended corrections, not a guarantee of any outcome.

Can you tell if my account is suspended by scanning my website?

No. A public website review cannot confirm private Merchant Center account status or the exact reason for an enforcement decision. We need the relevant issue details from your account to assess those questions.

Do you need my Google login?

No passwords are required. We can begin with your public storefront and the issue information you choose to share. If account access becomes necessary, we will discuss an appropriately authorized method separately.

Will you make changes to my store?

The initial service is an audit and action plan. Storefront, feed or account changes are separate work and require an agreed scope and permission.

Is How Work Runs affiliated with Google?

No. How Work Runs is an independent service provider and is not affiliated with, endorsed by or acting on behalf of Google.

What does a Google Merchant Center misrepresentation warning mean?

It is a policy-related concern about how a business, offer or transaction is represented. The exact notice and account context matter. A public checklist alone cannot establish why Google took an action, so we compare the warning with the storefront and information available from the merchant.

What is the difference between a product disapproval and an account suspension?

A product disapproval affects particular listings or destinations, while an account-level action can affect broader participation. The applicable notice determines the review scope and next steps.

Should I request another Google review immediately?

First identify the issue, make relevant corrections and consult the current instructions in Merchant Center. An independent audit can help organize the evidence, but Google controls its review process and timing.

Can you audit Shopify, WooCommerce or another e-commerce platform?

We can assess publicly visible storefront information regardless of platform and review product data you provide. Any platform-specific access or remediation work is assessed separately before we agree to it.

How much does the audit cost?

We confirm a fixed price after reviewing the issue and scope. You will know the cost before committing to paid work.
