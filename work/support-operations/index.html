Selected work · Support operations

Finding the work behind support costs

Ticket volume tells you how busy a support team is. It doesn't explain which problems are creating the work or which changes will reduce it.

Tell us what you're working onSee selected work

Context: Previous in-house operating work, shared in general terms. These are not How Work Runs client projects.

01

The situation

The team could see incoming support volume, but the reasons behind repeat contacts and their effects on staffing and cost were less clear. Without that detail, it was difficult to decide where process improvements, better guidance or automation would have the most impact.

02

What I changed

As support director, I partnered with Analytics to examine demand by request type, the work involved and where customers could reasonably resolve issues without contacting support. We used those findings to inform staffing, targets, process changes and automation. In a related effort, I examined the leading enterprise ticket category, introduced more consistent classification and worked with the team on clearer instructions, handoffs and escalation steps.

03

What changed

The broader support improvement effort delivered approximately $300,000 in realized savings, while customer satisfaction and first-response performance were above benchmark during that period. The previously leading enterprise ticket category also fell from the top spot within months.

04

What the result does—and doesn't—mean

The savings were realized across a broader operational improvement effort, not attributed to a single script or intervention. The useful lesson is that understanding the underlying work makes it possible to choose changes based on evidence instead of simply adding capacity or technology.

Results are drawn from prior operating roles, not engagements delivered by How Work Runs. Figures are rounded and contextualized rather than presented as guarantees.
