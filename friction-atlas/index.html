An open field guide / How Work Runs

Where work gets stuck, and what might help.

If your team spends hours chasing updates, copying information between tools or waiting for approvals, you're not alone. These ten examples show what that looks like in everyday work, what might be causing it and small changes worth trying before buying more software.

How to use the atlas

Understand the problem before buying a tool.

A report that takes half a day to assemble and an approval that sits in someone's inbox may seem unrelated, but both can come from unclear ownership, information stored in the wrong place or a process that has grown without anyone revisiting it. The examples here are designed to help you see where that happens, before deciding whether a new tool is necessary.

How this was assembled

About the examples

The businesses and situations are fictional, based on patterns that come up in operational work. The diagrams show one possible way to improve each process, not a measured client result or a promise of savings. Use them to ask better questions about your own workflows, and test any changes before relying on them.

The approach draws on established process-improvement and usability principles, including:

  • Lean Enterprise Institute: value-stream mapping: understanding current and future flows of work.
  • Project Management Institute: mapping your value stream: separating working time, waiting and loopbacks.
  • Nielsen Norman Group: usability heuristics: particularly status visibility and preventing errors.
  • Lean Enterprise Institute: Five Whys: looking beyond the immediate symptom.
