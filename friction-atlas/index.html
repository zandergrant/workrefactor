An open field guide / How Work Runs

Every business has friction. Here's where to look.

Ten familiar operational problems, unpacked with current-state sketches, possible redesigns and small experiments you can run with your team. Search for the problem you're seeing, then follow the work to its source.

A way to use the atlas

Look at the work before you choose a tool.

A repeated reminder, a hand-built report and a spreadsheet full of colored cells can look like unrelated problems. Often they're symptoms of unclear ownership, fragmented information or a process nobody has revisited in years. These entries are designed to help you investigate the cause before deciding what should change.

How this was assembled

Models to question, not numbers to quote.

The cases are fictional composites designed to make common operational problems concrete. The diagrams describe possible current and future states; they are not reports of measured client results, and no time or cost savings are asserted. The examples and recommendations are hypotheses to check against actual work, not forecasts or universal rules.

The approach draws on established process-improvement and usability principles, including:

  • Lean Enterprise Institute: value-stream mapping — understanding current and future flows of work.
  • Project Management Institute: mapping your value stream — separating working time, waiting and loopbacks.
  • Nielsen Norman Group: usability heuristics — particularly status visibility and preventing errors.
  • Lean Enterprise Institute: Five Whys — looking beyond the immediate symptom.
