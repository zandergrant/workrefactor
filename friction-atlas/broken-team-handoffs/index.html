How to Fix Team Handoffs That Keep Losing Information

Sales says a deal is finished, but delivery still needs to know what was promised, where the files are and who owns the next step. The work has moved from one team to another, although the receiving team isn't actually ready to begin.

What this looks like at work

A familiar example

In a fictional services company, sales signs a client and marks the deal as won. Delivery gets an email with the client's name and start date, but the agreed exceptions and promises from the negotiation aren't there. The first internal kickoff turns into a meeting to reconstruct what was sold.

An onboarding checklist would help if it captured what delivery really needs. I'd go a little further and give the receiving team a chance to accept the handoff, or send it back with a clear reason when something essential is missing. Otherwise incomplete work keeps moving downstream, where it becomes harder to fix.

What changes in the workflow?

I would define what 'ready for delivery' means with the people receiving the work, then make acceptance an explicit part of the handoff. That way, sales can finish its job and delivery can start its own without having to discover the missing pieces later.

Signs this is happening on your team

  • The receiving team regularly schedules meetings to find missing context.
  • A customer repeats information already shared during the sales process.
  • Work is marked done upstream but remains blocked downstream.

Where I'd start

I'd compare three handoffs that went smoothly with three that created delays, then ask the receiving team what they needed but didn't have. The goal is a short, useful set of requirements, not a form that copies every field available in the CRM.

What I'd try first

  1. Define ready

    Write the minimum conditions that make the next team able to start, including records, decisions, accountable owner and agreed first milestone.

  2. Capture commitments

    List exceptions and customer promises in a structured field with a link to the authoritative agreement.

  3. Introduce acceptance

    Allow the receiving owner to accept or return the handoff with a reason, so incomplete work does not quietly accumulate.

  4. Close the feedback loop

    Review returns with the sending team and remove fields that nobody uses while tightening information that routinely goes missing.

How to tell whether it helped

First-pass acceptanceShare of handoffs accepted without clarification or rework.
Time to readyElapsed time from upstream completion to accepted downstream start.
Customer repetitionHow often customers need to supply information already shared previously.

What to watch out for

One thing to watch

A form with thirty mandatory questions can turn a manageable process into a new administrative burden. Keep the required fields narrow, protect sensitive contract details, and make sure the acceptance step is a real decision rather than a checkbox everyone clicks automatically.

When a simpler fix is enough

When every engagement is different, I'd keep the common requirements short and leave space for case-specific commitments. A detailed form that treats every customer the same can lose the very details the next team needs.

One change to test this week

Try a five-field handoff brief for two weeks and ask the receiving owner to accept or return each one. Keep a record of what still requires clarification, then revise the brief around those real cases rather than treating the first draft as final.
