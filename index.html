The problem

As your team grows, small workarounds can start slowing everyone down.

Maybe the same customer details are entered three times, an approval takes a week, or nobody knows which report is correct. Those small frustrations add up as a business grows. These are the warning signs I look for.

  • Too many spreadsheets
  • Information copied between tools by hand
  • Repetitive reporting
  • Approvals buried in inboxes
  • Processes that live in one person’s memory
  • Disconnected systems
  • Unclear ownership
  • Poor visibility into work
  • Workflows that break as volume grows
  • New software added without fixing the process

Selected work

Making complicated work easier to run.

These examples come from my operating work: the problems teams were dealing with, what I built using the tools already available, and how their day-to-day work changed.

A single place to see whether a launch is actually ready

Before
Staffing, training, deadlines and capacity all lived in different places. Getting a reliable answer about launch readiness meant collecting updates from several teams and checking them by hand.
My part
Using the systems already in place, I built a secure internal web application that brought information from different teams, reporting tools and training systems into one shared view. Automated data pulls and accuracy checks kept it current, while teams could continue making updates through familiar workflows. No separate software subscription or lengthy implementation was needed.

After

One dependable view of readiness, without asking every team for another update.

Teams could check staffing, training, milestones and risks together, catch missing information earlier, and make decisions from the same up-to-date picture.

Making thousands of global live events easy to navigate

Before
Thousands of events were being delivered worldwide. Employees needed a reliable way to see what was happening, who was assigned and what had changed without piecing together plans and calendars.
My part
I built a system that turned delivery plans into an organized, easy-to-review schedule of live events across regions and time zones. It used existing spreadsheets and calendars, automatically handled changes and assignments, and checked for duplicate events instead of relying on staff to reconcile each update.

After

Thousands of events, one clear view.

Employees could review schedules and assignments in one place, while changes flowed through the existing tools instead of creating another round of manual coordination.

Cutting support costs by understanding what was driving the work

Before
We knew how many support tickets came in, but not which recurring problems, types of work or staffing decisions were pushing costs up.
My part
As support director, I worked with Analytics to understand where demand came from, which requests customers could resolve on their own, and how different types of work affected staffing and cost. We used those findings to improve staffing plans, targets and automation.

After

≈$300K

Realized savings from the broader improvement effort, with customer satisfaction and first-response performance also above benchmark during that period.

Finding and fixing the reasons customers kept contacting support

Before
One issue kept bringing enterprise customers back to support. The ticket totals showed the scale of the problem, but not what was causing it.
My part
I examined the requests themselves, built a way to categorize them consistently, and worked with the team to change the instructions, handoffs and escalation steps that were creating repeat contact.

After

The issue that generated the most enterprise requests stopped leading the list.

Within months, the leading ticket category had fallen from the top spot. The team also had clearer evidence of which recurring problems to address next.

How we work

Start with the people and the work, then the tools.

We talk to the people doing the work, watch how it actually gets done and look at the evidence before deciding what to change.

Understand the work

We follow the process with the people who use it, looking at where information gets lost, decisions stall, or someone has to step in to keep things moving.

Simplify the process

Together, we work out which steps are useful, what can be removed, and where ownership needs to be clearer, making the most of the systems you already have.

Test and improve

We put a manageable change into practice, look at what the data and the people doing the work tell us, and refine it before expanding it further.

The aim is to give your team more room for focused work, with fewer things to maintain. We build around how people actually work and keep improving the process as we learn what helps.
